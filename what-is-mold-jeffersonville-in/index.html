What Is Mold? A Jeffersonville, IN Homeowner Guide

By the Jeffersonville Water Damage Pros team

Mold is a common fungus that grows indoors when moisture lingers. Here is what it is, where it hides and what to do next.

☎ Call (812) 642-7696

Mold is a type of fungus that grows on damp organic material, from drywall paper to wood to carpet backing. Spores are always in the air, indoors and out, and they only grow where there is moisture, a food source and time. Take away the moisture and the growth stops.

This guide explains what mold is and how to respond. It is not medical advice, and questions about symptoms belong with a physician. If you already have growth, start with the mold remediation page.

What mold needs to grow

Moisture is the key. A leak, a flood, a humid room or condensation on cold surfaces can all supply it. Materials that stay wet for more than a day or two are at risk, which is why fast drying matters.

Mold can begin to grow quickly in materials that stay wet, which is why drying matters more than disinfecting.

Where mold hides in houses

  • Behind baseboards and under sinks after leaks.
  • On the paper face of drywall.
  • In basements and crawl spaces with poor airflow.
  • On ceilings below bathrooms or roof leaks.
  • Inside HVAC drain pans and ducts.

Look for staining, fuzzy or speckled patches and a musty smell. A smell with no visible source often means growth inside a wall or under flooring.

What to do if you find mold

  1. Fix the moisture source first.
  2. Keep the area dry with air movement and a dehumidifier.
  3. Do not disturb large areas, and do not paint over them.
  4. Take photos and note when the water event happened.
  5. Call a professional for growth larger than a small patch or growth inside walls.

A small spot on a hard surface can be cleaned by a homeowner, but growth on drywall or larger areas is a job for a crew.

Mold, water damage and drying

Most indoor mold problems start with water damage that was not fully dried. That is why we treat drying as the main prevention tool. A floor that looks dry can have a wet subfloor, and a moisture meter is the only reliable test.

If you recently had a leak, ask for moisture readings before you close walls. The structural drying page explains how readings are used.

Mold, mildew and other lookalikes

Not every dark patch is mold. Soot, dirt, dust and stains from old leaks can look similar. A few clues help you decide whether to worry.

CluePoints toward moldPoints toward something else
TextureFuzzy, slimy or speckledFlat, dry stain with a clear edge
SmellMusty or earthyNo smell, or a chemical smell
Moisture nearbyLeak, condensation or damp airDry surroundings
SpreadGrows or returns over weeksStays the same size
LocationCorners, under sinks, behind furnitureNear vents or candles (soot or dust)

When you are not sure, a moisture meter reading and a close look by a professional are better than guessing.

Simple habits that keep moisture down

  • Use bathroom and kitchen exhaust fans during and after use.
  • Run a dehumidifier in damp basements.
  • Fix drips under sinks and around tubs as soon as you see them.
  • Keep gutters and downspouts directing water away from the foundation.
  • Do not leave wet towels or laundry in a pile.

None of this requires special equipment, and each step removes one of the three things mold needs to start.

When to call a professional

Call a crew when the growth is larger than a small patch, when it returns after cleaning, when it follows a flood or sewage backup, or when you smell it but cannot find it. A crew can open a wall safely, contain the dust and dry the cavity.

Call a doctor, not a contractor, for questions about symptoms. Mold affects people differently, and a restoration company is not the place for health advice.

  • Growth after any water damage that was not dried within a day or two.
  • A musty smell that persists after you clean and dry.
  • Visible growth on drywall, ceiling tiles or insulation.
  • Mold on materials in a crawl space or attic.

A short call is usually enough to decide what the next step should be.

Common myths

  • Myth: all black mold is toxic. Fact: color alone does not tell you the risk, and identifying a type takes a lab.
  • Myth: bleach cures mold. Fact: it does not fix moisture or reach into porous materials.
  • Myth: a dry-looking wall is safe. Fact: moisture can remain inside the wall.
  • Myth: mold only grows in dirty houses. Fact: it grows wherever moisture is present, clean or not.

Sorting fact from myth helps you spend money where it counts, which is on finding and fixing moisture.

Mold and Jeffersonville homes

Jeffersonville's older neighborhoods, including the Old Jeffersonville Historic District listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1987, have many older houses. Older masonry and plaster can hold moisture, so damp spots can linger.

Water supplied to the city comes from wells and measured 182 ppm hardness in the utility's 2024 report. That has nothing to do with mold, but it does mean scale builds up on fixtures, so check seals and supply lines when you service them.

Frequently asked questions

Is all mold black?

No. Mold can be white, green, gray, black or other colors. Color does not tell you how risky it is.

Can I tell mold from dirt?

Mold usually looks fuzzy or speckled and comes with moisture. A moisture meter and a visual check help.

Will vinegar or bleach fix it?

They may clean surface growth on hard materials, but they do not fix moisture and are not reliable on porous materials.

Should I test my home?

Not by default. Testing is useful in specific situations, such as after remediation.

What is the most common cause in Jeffersonville homes?

Leaks and damp basements are common causes anywhere. The key is finding the moisture.
