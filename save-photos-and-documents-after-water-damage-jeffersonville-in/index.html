How to Save Photos and Documents After Water Damage in Jeffersonville, IN

By the Jeffersonville Water Damage Pros team

Wet photos and papers cannot be replaced, which is why they deserve attention in the first day.

☎ Call (812) 642-7696

Furniture can be bought again. Family photos, birth certificates and a stack of old letters cannot. When water reaches paper, time matters, because wet paper sticks together, ink runs and mold begins.

This guide covers first steps for photos, books and documents. It is general advice, not conservation work. Valuable or irreplaceable items may be best sent to a professional document recovery service.

First steps for any wet paper

  1. Do not pull stuck pages apart. Let them soak free gently or dry first.
  2. Handle by the edges and keep items flat.
  3. Move them to a clean, dry area away from direct sunlight.
  4. If you cannot dry them quickly, freeze them, which stops mold and buys time.
  5. Photograph or scan important documents before further handling if you can.

The freezer is the most useful trick. A sealed bag in the freezer holds the damage while you decide what to do, and professionals use the same method for large loads.

Photographs

Photographs are best rinsed gently in clean water if they are muddy, then laid face up on a clean, absorbent surface to dry. Do not stack them while wet, because they stick together and tear when separated.

  • Rinse gently without rubbing the image surface.
  • Dry flat on paper towels, a clean cloth or a screen.
  • Keep them out of the sun.
  • Replace towels as they get wet.
  • Do not use hair dryers or heat.

If photos have already stuck to glass or each other, freeze them and talk to a conservator. Do not force them apart.

Books, papers and important documents

For important documents, request replacements from the issuing agency as a backup. Many can be reissued, which takes pressure off the wet originals.

Water and mold

Wet paper grows mold quickly, so dry it or freeze it fast. Mold-stained items should be handled with gloves and a mask. If you see growth on documents, do not try to clean them yourself. Place them in a bag and ask a professional.

If the house itself is wet, drying the structure also matters, since damp air will keep re-wetting paper. See our page on structural drying.

Preventing the next loss

  • Store important papers in waterproof, fire-rated containers above floor level.
  • Keep digital copies in cloud storage or on a drive stored elsewhere.
  • Do not store papers in basements or against exterior walls.
  • Scan photos you care about.
  • Keep a list of what you own and where documents are kept.

Many people only think about this after a loss. A weekend afternoon with a scanner is worth far more than the best recovery effort.

Who can help with rare or large collections

Document recovery services use vacuum freeze-drying and other methods for large or valuable collections. Libraries, archives and museums sometimes offer advice, and professional conservators can help with fragile prints and old books.

Ask your insurer whether your policy covers document recovery and whether it has a limit for papers and photographs. Some policies have low limits for these items, so it helps to know before you pay for a service.

  • Gather the items in clean containers lined with paper.
  • Label boxes with the room and date.
  • Freeze them in sealed bags if there will be a delay.
  • Write down what is in each box.
  • Ask the service how they want items packed before you ship or deliver them.

Do this while the structure itself is being dried. Documents kept in a damp house will keep absorbing moisture, so move them to a dry place as soon as you can.

If you have a choice, prioritize what is irreplaceable. Insurance can pay for a new sofa, but nothing replaces a photograph of a grandparent. Work on those first, and let the rest wait.

Digital habits that protect memories

Scanning a photo takes a minute, and a cloud backup keeps it safe from fire, flood and theft. Many people never do this until they have a loss. Start with the photos and papers you would grieve most, and work outward.

Name files so you can find them later, and keep a copy at a relative's home or in cloud storage. A little time spent now makes the next emergency much less painful.

Records you may need in Jeffersonville

If your property is in a Special Flood Hazard Area, the city Building Commissioner's office at 500 Quartermaster Court, (812) 285-6412, keeps flood maps and elevation records. Copies of your insurance policy and mortgage papers should be stored where water cannot reach them.

If you lose original records in a flood, ask the issuing agency for replacements. County offices and state agencies can reissue many documents on request.

Frequently asked questions

Can wet photos be saved?

Often, if you act quickly. Rinse gently, dry flat and keep them from sticking.

Why freeze wet papers?

Freezing stops mold and keeps pages from sticking while you plan.

Can I use a hair dryer on wet papers?

No. Heat can warp paper and photos and damage ink.

What if the papers are moldy?

Handle with gloves and a mask and ask a professional for help.

Is there a service for large loads?

Document recovery services handle large or valuable collections. Ask your insurer or restoration company for referrals.
