How to Find a Hidden Water Leak in Your Jeffersonville, IN Home

By the Jeffersonville Water Damage Pros team

Hidden leaks do the most damage because nobody sees them. These checks help you find one before it finds you.

☎ Call (812) 642-7696

A hidden leak is any leak you cannot see directly: a pinhole in a supply line inside a wall, a drain that weeps under a slab, a roof leak that runs along a rafter before it drips. They often run for weeks before a stain, a smell or a bill gives them away.

You do not need special tools for the first round of checks. This guide starts with the free and simple ones and ends with the signs that mean it is time to call a plumber or a restoration crew.

Start with the water meter

Turn off every faucet and appliance that uses water. Look at your water meter. If the dial or the small leak indicator is moving, water is going somewhere. Note the reading, wait 30 minutes without using any water, and read it again.

If the number changed, you have a leak between the meter and your fixtures. Also check the toilets, since a silent toilet leak is the most common household leak. A few drops of food coloring in the tank will show up in the bowl within minutes if the flapper is leaking.

Look at your bill

Compare recent water bills with the same months last year. A sudden rise without a change in how you live is a signal. Bills are not proof, since rates and seasons change, but they are a good reason to run the meter test.

Check the bill for the usage amount, not only the total. If the usage rose while the household stayed the same, take the meter test.

Walk the house with your senses

  • Smell for musty, earthy odors in closets, basements and under sinks.
  • Look for stains, bubbling paint, warped baseboards or lifted flooring.
  • Feel walls and floors for cool or damp spots.
  • Listen at night for hissing or running water when everything is off.
  • Check the ceiling below bathrooms and above kitchens.

Write down where you find something. Several clues in one area point to a source nearby.

What to do once you find it

  1. Shut off the water to the fixture, or at the main shutoff if you cannot find the valve.
  2. Take photos before touching anything.
  3. Call a plumber to repair the pipe or fixture.
  4. Dry wet materials within a day or two so that mold does not start.
  5. Call your insurance agent if there is damage beyond the leak itself.

A repaired pipe is only half of the fix. Wet drywall, insulation and flooring stay wet until someone dries them. A moisture meter reading shows what is still wet.

Tools worth having

  • A flashlight for looking under sinks and in attics and crawl spaces.
  • A small mirror to see behind appliances.
  • Plug-in or battery water sensors placed under sinks and near the water heater.
  • A moisture meter, which is inexpensive and shows damp drywall and wood.
  • A notebook or phone to record meter readings and dates.

Sensors are inexpensive and send a warning before a small leak turns into a large one. Place them where leaks are most likely: under kitchen and bathroom sinks, behind the toilet, next to the washing machine and by the water heater.

If you travel often, a smart shutoff valve that stops the water when it detects flow in an unusual pattern is another option. That is a plumbing decision, so ask a plumber what suits your home.

Whatever you choose, test it. A sensor with a dead battery will not warn you of anything.

When to call a plumber

Call a plumber when the meter moves with everything off, when you hear water inside a wall, when a slab floor feels warm, or when you find a wet spot but cannot find where it starts. A plumber has listening devices, cameras and pressure tests that locate leaks without cutting into walls unnecessarily.

Call a restoration company as well once the leak has wet walls, floors or ceilings. The plumber fixes the pipe, and the crew dries the building so mold does not start.

Frequently asked questions

How much water can a small leak waste?

It depends on the size, but even a slow drip adds up over weeks. The meter and bill will show it.

Can a leak be inside a wall with no stain?

Yes. A pinhole leak can wet insulation and framing for a long time before anything shows on the surface.

Who should I call first?

Shut off the water, then call a plumber to fix the pipe. Call a restoration company to dry what got wet.

Do leak detection devices help?

Water sensors under sinks and near the water heater can warn you early. They do not replace repair.

Will insurance cover a hidden leak?

It may cover sudden damage, and gradual seepage is often excluded. Ask your agent.
