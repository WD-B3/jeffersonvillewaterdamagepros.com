Water Damage Restoration in the Old Jeffersonville Historic District, IN

Older masonry, plaster and basements hold water longer than newer homes. We dry them with readings, not guesses.

☎ Call (812) 642-7696

The Old Jeffersonville Historic District is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. The National Park Service record gives the boundary as roughly Court Avenue, Graham Street, the Ohio River and Interstate 65. The district covers about 203 acres and roughly 500 buildings, and most of the buildings still standing were built after 1870.

People live, work and worship in these buildings, so a leak here can mean a family home, a flat above a shop or a historic building with plaster and brick that need gentle handling. This page is for water damage in homes and small buildings within those streets.

Streets and building types in the district

Spring Street is the main road through the district, with secondary streets running north from the river, including Riverside Drive, Market Street, Chestnut Street, Maple Street and Court Avenue. Commercial buildings along Spring Street are mostly Italianate and eclectic Victorian, typically two to three stories of brick. The residential blocks include American Foursquare houses, bungalows and shotgun houses.

These are different buildings with different weak points. We look at each one before we plan the drying.

How water behaves in older buildings

  • Solid brick and plaster absorb water and release it slowly, so a wall that looks dry can still be wet inside.
  • Shotgun houses are narrow and long, so water from a roof or wall leak can travel the length of the house along floor framing.
  • Foursquare houses stack rooms in two full floors, which means a failure upstairs often soaks the ceiling below and keeps going down.
  • Basements under older buildings can have stone or brick walls that wick moisture from the ground and stay damp after the visible water is gone.
  • Old finishes, such as plaster, lath and original trim, need to be dried carefully rather than torn out unless readings show they must be.

Older sewers and basement water

According to the city, Jeffersonville has combined sewers dating to about 1900 that serve much of the older downtown. In heavy rain, older systems can be overwhelmed and water can back up into low areas. We cannot tell from the street whether your building is on such a line, so ask the city if you want to know. If sewage has come into your building, stay out and do not dry it yourself.

First steps, safely

  1. Shut off the water source if you can reach it without standing in water.
  2. Do not touch a breaker or switch if the floor around it is wet. Call an electrician.
  3. Photograph the damage, including ceilings, plaster and trim, before anything is moved.
  4. Open windows only if it is dry outside and it is safe to do so.
  5. Call us and describe the building, the source and how long the water has been there.

Extraction, drying and your claim

We pull standing water, set drying equipment and measure moisture at intervals until the building reads dry. In older buildings we record readings in several places, since one dry reading does not prove the next wall is dry. Our water extraction and structural drying pages explain the steps.

We document the loss with photos, readings and a written scope of work you can share with your insurer. We do not promise coverage or tell you what your policy will pay. If your building has historic status, ask your insurer and the city whether any review applies before you replace exterior features.
