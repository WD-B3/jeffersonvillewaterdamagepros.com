What to Keep or Throw Away After Water Damage in Jeffersonville, IN

By the Jeffersonville Water Damage Pros team

Deciding what to save is hard when you are tired and the house is wet. Water type and time are your two best guides.

☎ Call (812) 642-7696

Water damage forces a stream of small decisions: this rug, that sofa, a box of photos. The right choice depends on how dirty the water was, how long the item stayed wet and what it is made of. Keeping a ruined item can expose you to mold and bacteria, and throwing out a salvageable one costs money.

Photograph everything before you throw anything away, and keep a list for your insurer. Those two steps protect your claim whichever way you decide.

Start with the water type

If the water came from sewage, a backed-up drain or a flood, porous items that touched it are generally discarded. If it came from a clean source and was dried quickly, many items can be saved.

Items that usually can be saved

  • Metal, glass and hard plastic after cleaning and sanitizing.
  • Solid wood furniture that dried quickly.
  • Dishes and cookware, washed and sanitized.
  • Clothing and linens from clean water, washed promptly.
  • Many electronics only after a professional looks at them. Do not plug in wet equipment.

When in doubt, set the item aside rather than discarding it, and photograph it so that you have a record.

Items that usually go

  • Anything porous that touched sewage or flood water.
  • Food, medicine and cosmetics that touched water.
  • Mattresses and pillows that soaked through.
  • Particleboard furniture that has swelled.
  • Carpet pad and wet insulation.
  • Stuffed toys and soft items with dirty water exposure.

For large pieces, ask your restoration company before you haul things to the curb. Some items must be inspected by an adjuster first. Our flood restoration page explains how larger losses are handled.

Keep a record as you go

  1. Photograph each item and its damage.
  2. Write down its name, age and approximate value.
  3. Keep a few samples of carpet or flooring if the adjuster asks.
  4. Keep receipts for replacement and cleaning.
  5. Store salvageable items somewhere dry.

A spreadsheet or notebook is enough. Include the room where each item was.

Be careful with salvage

Wear gloves and a mask when handling wet or moldy items. Do not mix cleaning products. Do not use food or medicine that touched flood water, and do not run appliances that were submerged until a technician checks them.

If a large number of items were affected, a professional contents cleaning service can handle sorting and cleaning. Ask your restoration company whether it provides that or can refer you.

Sentimental items and heirlooms

Some wet items matter far more than their price. Quilts, wooden furniture, photographs and keepsakes may be worth professional cleaning even when replacement would be cheaper. Set them aside, dry them gently and ask a specialist before you decide. Textile and furniture restorers can often save what a general cleaner cannot.

Do not rush these decisions. Take photos, store the items somewhere dry and revisit them once the immediate emergency has passed.

Cleaning what you keep

Hard items are washed with warm water and detergent, rinsed and sanitized, then dried completely. Clothing and linens from clean water go through a hot wash. Items you are unsure about can be photographed, bagged and held until an adjuster or crew advises you.

  1. Wash hard items with detergent and warm water.
  2. Rinse with clean water.
  3. Sanitize with a product made for the surface.
  4. Dry thoroughly in open air or with a dehumidifier.
  5. Store only when completely dry.

Avoid mixing cleaning chemicals, and wear gloves and eye protection. Dry everything fully before it goes back into a closet or box.

Disposal and curbside rules

Large amounts of wet debris may exceed what regular trash pickup takes. Ask your trash hauler or the city about bulk pickup rules before you stack items at the curb, and keep hazardous items such as batteries and chemicals separate.

If you hired a restoration company, ask whether it handles debris removal as part of the job, and confirm whether that is included or billed separately.

If you are throwing away a large number of items, take a wide photo of the pile before it leaves. It supports your list for the insurer and shows the volume of the loss.

Flood-prone areas around Jeffersonville

Jeffersonville has combined sewers in the older downtown area, about 989 acres from Main Street west to the Clarksville line, according to the city. During heavy rain, those systems can be overwhelmed, so a basement in that part of town may take in dirty water.

If water came from a sewer or the river, treat it as contaminated and discard porous items that were exposed. The Storm Water Department at (812) 285-6476 can answer questions about local drainage.

Frequently asked questions

Can a couch be saved after water damage?

Sometimes, if the water was clean and it dried quickly. Dirty water usually means disposal.

Is it safe to keep wet clothes?

Wash clean-water items right away. Discard items that touched sewage unless a cleaner says otherwise.

What about wet electronics?

Do not turn them on. Have them checked by a technician.

Should I keep damaged items for the adjuster?

When practical, yes, or keep clear photos and a list.

Who can help with a big cleanup?

Your restoration company can advise on contents, and some offer pack-out services.
