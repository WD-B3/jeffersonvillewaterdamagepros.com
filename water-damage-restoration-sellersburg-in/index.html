Why Sellersburg homeowners call us

Common calls from Sellersburg include burst pipes, water heater failures, basement seepage after heavy rain and ceiling leaks from roofs. In each case, the water that is visible is only part of the story, because it moves into walls, insulation and subfloors.

Permits, utilities and who to ask

Sellersburg has its own Building Department at 316 E Utica Street, (812) 246-3821, and its own Sellersburg Water Utility and Waste Water pages. If a repair needs a permit, or if you have questions about water or sewer service, the town offices are the right place to ask.

The town adopted a Storm Water Ordinance in January 2021, so drainage and runoff on your property may be subject to local rules. If your water problem comes from drainage, ask the town before you change grading or add drains.

Urgent steps during an emergency

In an emergency, the most useful thing you can do is stop the water. Find the main shutoff, turn it off and check for water near electrical items. If water is pouring in from outside, call us and the town, since the source may be public.

Before We Arrive

Find the shutoff

Turn the main off if water is still running. A shutoff valve near the meter or where the line enters the house is usually the one.

Cut power only if dry

If the panel is in a dry spot, you may switch off wet circuits. If not, stay out and tell us.

Move valuables up

Stack boxes, books and electronics on a table, a shelf or an upper floor.

Avoid contaminated water

Water with an odor or a dark color should not be touched with bare hands. Keep pets away.

Record the damage

Snap the water line on walls, the ceiling stain and any failed fitting, before anything moves.

Phone the 24/7 line

Call any hour and tell us what you see. We start planning while you finish these steps.

What to expect on the first visit

The crew confirms the source is stopped, removes standing water and takes meter readings in walls, floors and ceilings. We walk through what will be dried in place and what may come out, and we show you the numbers so the plan is clear.

Drying equipment runs for days, and a technician returns to check readings. You receive photos and a written scope you can share with your insurer, and we explain the price before work starts.

After the water is out

Drying is where most of the work happens. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for days, and a technician checks readings until walls and floors match dry areas. Skipping this step is how mold and warped floors show up weeks later.

If the water was dirty, items that touched it are usually discarded. If it was clean and dried quickly, many items can be saved. We tell you which is which, and we write the scope so you can share it with your adjuster. Neighbors in Clarksville and Henryville follow the same process.
