Mold vs Mildew: What Jeffersonville, IN Homeowners Should Know

By the Jeffersonville Water Damage Pros team

People use the two words as if they mean the same thing. They overlap, but the difference helps you decide how to respond.

☎ Call (812) 642-7696

Mildew and mold are both fungi that grow where moisture lingers. In everyday talk, mildew refers to a flat, powdery growth on the surface of a material, while mold is used for thicker, fuzzier growth that can reach into what it grows on. The two words are not always used the same way, even among professionals.

The more useful question is how deep the problem goes and why it is there. This guide gives you simple ways to judge that. It is not medical advice, and anyone with health concerns about mold should speak with a doctor.

How they usually look

FeatureMildew (common use)Mold (common use)
TextureFlat, powdery or downyFuzzy, slimy or raised
ColorWhite, gray or pale yellowGreen, black, blue or many colors
Where it growsOn the surface of damp materialOn and into porous material
Typical placesShower walls, window sills, fabricDrywall, wood, insulation, carpet backing
CleaningOften wiped off a hard surfaceMay need removal of the material

These are guidelines, not a diagnosis. Color in particular is not a reliable guide, since the same growth can look different at different stages.

Why the difference matters for cleanup

Surface growth on a hard, nonporous surface such as tile, glass or sealed fixtures can be wiped away with detergent and water, then dried. A small patch of this kind is a housekeeping job.

Growth that has penetrated drywall, ceiling tile, insulation or carpet is a different matter, because the material is porous and the fungus has grown into it. Wiping the face does not remove what is inside. Those materials are usually removed. Read more on the mold remediation page.

What they have in common

  • Both need moisture to grow.
  • Both return if the moisture source is not fixed.
  • Both feed on organic material such as paper, wood, dust and soap film.
  • Both can be reduced by lower humidity and better airflow.

That last point is why prevention is the same for both. Keep bathrooms ventilated, fix leaks and keep basements and crawl spaces dry.

Where to look in your home

Mildew tends to show up where a surface gets wet and stays humid, such as shower corners, window frames with condensation and the underside of a bathroom ceiling. Mold appears where water has soaked in, such as behind a leaking sink, under a wet carpet or on the back of drywall.

If a stain keeps coming back after wiping, treat it as a moisture clue, not a cleaning failure. Something nearby is staying wet, and finding it is the real job. A search for hidden leaks often starts with the places where stains return.

A quick decision guide

  1. Is the growth on a hard, sealed surface and smaller than a few square feet? Clean and dry it.
  2. Is it on drywall, ceiling tile, carpet or insulation? Plan to remove the material.
  3. Did it follow a leak, a flood or sewage? Call a professional.
  4. Does it return after cleaning? Look for the moisture source.
  5. Does anyone in the home have health concerns? Talk to a doctor and avoid disturbing it.

When in doubt, take a photo and call. Describing what you see is usually enough for us to suggest the next step.

Prevention that works for both

You can lower the odds of either by keeping indoor air drier and moving. These steps cost little and make a difference over a year.

  • Run bathroom fans for 20 to 30 minutes after a shower.
  • Vent clothes dryers outdoors, not into the house.
  • Use a dehumidifier in basements during humid months.
  • Wipe condensation from windows and sills.
  • Keep furniture a few inches from exterior walls so air can move.
  • Repair roof, gutter and plumbing leaks promptly.

Sealing and grout maintenance in bathrooms also matter, because cracked caulk lets water behind tile. If you see growth in the same spot repeatedly, check for a leak behind it. A single bathroom wall that keeps growing spots may have a failed seal or a plumbing drip, and a crew can check with a meter.

None of these steps eliminates spores, which are always present. They just take away the moisture that growth needs.

A note on testing and labels

Homeowners sometimes buy a kit to find out whether a patch is mold or mildew. The result rarely changes what you should do, since both are handled by removing the growth and the moisture. Spend that effort on finding the source of water instead.

If a lab identification is needed for a legal or medical reason, a qualified assessor can arrange it. Otherwise, treat any recurring growth as a moisture problem to solve.

Humidity in the Ohio Valley

Jeffersonville sits on the Ohio River, where summers are humid and basements can stay damp when it rains. Surface growth is common in bathrooms and tends to show up when ventilation is poor.

Water supplied by Indiana American Water to Jeffersonville comes from groundwater wells, with a reported hardness of 182 ppm in 2024. Regular cleaning and drying of shower walls still matters whatever the water hardness.

Frequently asked questions

Is mildew less serious than mold?

On a hard surface it is usually simpler to clean. The cause, moisture, is the same, so fix it either way.

Can I clean mildew myself?

Usually, on small hard surfaces, with detergent, protective gear and good drying.

Does bleach kill mold?

Bleach is not reliable on porous materials and does not solve moisture. Detergent and drying are the usual approach for small hard-surface spots.

Why does the growth keep coming back?

Moisture is still present. Look for leaks, condensation or poor ventilation.

Should I get a test?

Testing is not necessary for visible growth. Fix the moisture and remove the material.
