Water Damage Insurance Claim Steps in Jeffersonville, IN

By the Jeffersonville Water Damage Pros team

A water damage claim goes better when you document early and keep good records. Here are the steps, in order, with local contacts.

☎ Call (812) 642-7696

Filing an insurance claim after water damage feels like a second emergency. The steps are not complicated, but they have to be done in the right order. Protect the property, record what happened, report the loss and keep every receipt.

This guide covers the process, not whether your claim will be paid. That is up to your policy and your insurer, and we never promise an outcome. If you are still deciding whether to file, find out first whether your policy covers the cause of the loss.

Step 1: stop the loss and stay safe

Shut off the water source and, if it is safe, the power to wet areas. Your policy expects you to protect the property from further damage. Emergency measures such as extraction and drying are usually part of that duty, and the receipts matter.

If the water is contaminated or near live wiring, do not enter. Our sewage backup page explains why.

Step 2: document before you clean

  • Take wide photos of each room, then close-ups of damage and the water line.
  • Record a slow video walk-through while narrating what you see.
  • Photograph serial numbers and labels on damaged appliances.
  • Keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them, when practical.
  • Write down the time you found the water and what you did.

We take moisture readings and thermal images and share a written scope. These do not replace your photos, but they add technical evidence.

Step 3: report the loss

Call your agent or the claims line on your policy. Ask for a claim number, who your adjuster is and what they need from you. Keep notes of every call with date, name and what was said.

A list of damaged property with its age and approximate value helps the adjuster, and that applies to water damage claims of every type.

Step 4: mitigation and repair

Do not delay drying while you wait. Ask your insurer whether it has a preferred contractor, and know that you can usually choose your own. Keep a copy of the written scope and the daily moisture readings from the drying process.

Before major repairs, get written approval or an estimate agreement from the insurer if your policy requires it. Save samples of removed materials if the adjuster asks.

Step 5: follow up

Keep copies of every document. If a payment does not cover the scope, ask for the itemized explanation. The Indiana Department of Insurance offers consumer help with insurance disputes.

Flood policies have their own deadlines and paperwork, so ask your flood insurer what is required and by when.

A simple claim log you can keep

A claim can run for weeks, and memory fades. A one-page log keeps the facts straight and makes follow-up calls shorter.

Keep the log with the photos in one folder, on paper or on your phone, so you can hand the whole set to an adjuster if asked.

What your restoration company should and should not do

A restoration company can document the loss, extract water, dry the structure and write a scope of work. It cannot decide coverage, and it should not promise that your insurer will pay.

  • It should explain the scope in writing before work begins.
  • It should share readings and photos you can forward to the adjuster.
  • It should tell you what it can and cannot control.
  • It should never pressure you to sign away your claim rights.

If anyone tells you the claim will definitely be approved, treat that as a warning sign.

Questions to ask your adjuster

  1. What is my deductible for this type of loss?
  2. Does my policy cover temporary housing if the home is unlivable?
  3. Is emergency mitigation reimbursed, and what receipts do you need?
  4. Will you inspect before drying equipment is removed?
  5. How will contents, such as furniture and electronics, be valued?
  6. What is the process if I disagree with the estimate?

Write down the answers and the name of the person who gave them. Adjusters handle many claims, and your notes keep the conversation moving.

If the adjuster cannot visit right away, ask whether photos and the restoration company's documentation can be used in the meantime. Do not let the home sit wet while you wait for an appointment.

Keeping the claim moving

Claims stall when paperwork goes missing. Send documents by email so you have a record, and follow up by phone if you do not hear back within a few business days. Be polite and specific, and always refer to your claim number.

Jeffersonville contacts that can help

The city Building Commissioner's office at 500 Quartermaster Court, (812) 285-6412, keeps flood maps and elevation certificates. If your property is in a Special Flood Hazard Area, those records can matter to a flood claim.

If repairs are large, the city says that a rebuild costing more than 50 percent of the building's market value, excluding land, triggers current floodplain standards. Ask about that before you sign a rebuild contract.

Frequently asked questions

How soon must I report water damage?

Policies usually require prompt notice. Call as soon as you can and ask your agent about deadlines.

Can I start repairs before the adjuster comes?

Emergency mitigation is expected. Major repairs may need approval. Ask your adjuster.

Do I have to use my insurer's contractor?

Usually not. Ask your agent to confirm.

What if my claim is denied?

Ask for the reason in writing and call the Indiana Department of Insurance for help.

Do I need a Jeffersonville permit to rebuild?

Possibly. Floodplain properties may need a city permit. Call the Building Commissioner's office.
