What Can You Dry Yourself After Water Damage in Jeffersonville, IN?

By the Jeffersonville Water Damage Pros team

A few small wet items you can handle at home. Wet walls, floors and anything touched by sewage are different.

☎ Call (812) 642-7696

After a small spill or a minor leak, you can often dry things yourself. After a bigger event, hidden moisture can keep a structure wet for weeks. This guide sorts out which jobs are safe for a homeowner and which need professional equipment.

The key question is the water source. Clean water from a supply line is a different matter from water that has touched sewage, a flooded yard or a drain. When the water is dirty, do not try to handle it yourself.

Check the water first

Water typeExamplesDo it yourself?
CleanSupply line, rain through a window, sink overflowOften, for small amounts
GrayWashing machine or dishwasher dischargeUse caution, call if large
BlackSewage, river or flood waterNo. Call a professional

If you are unsure, assume the water is dirtier than it looks. See sewage backup cleanup for why dirty water is handled differently.

What you can usually dry at home

  • Small spills on hard floors, mopped and dried right away.
  • Wet towels, clothing and bedding from clean water, washed and dried quickly.
  • Small wet areas on carpet, if lifted, dried and checked underneath.
  • Furniture with hard surfaces, wiped and dried.
  • Books and papers, if only slightly damp, interleaved with paper towels.

Move quickly. Run fans and a dehumidifier, open windows if the outdoor air is drier than the indoor air, and raise furniture off wet carpet.

What to leave to a crew

  • Walls and insulation that have soaked up water.
  • Ceilings that sag or stain.
  • Subfloors and anything under hardwood or tile.
  • Anything wet for more than a day or two.
  • Carpet pad, which usually cannot be dried in place.
  • Basement or crawl space flooding.

These areas hold water out of sight. A surface can feel dry while the framing or subfloor is wet, and that is where mold grows. A crew uses meters and drying equipment designed for this. Read about structural drying and carpet restoration.

A simple drying plan

  1. Stop the source of water.
  2. Remove standing water with a mop, towels or a wet vacuum.
  3. Lift or move wet items to a dry area.
  4. Run fans and a dehumidifier together in a closed room.
  5. Check with a moisture meter after a day, and keep going until readings match dry areas.

If readings do not fall after a day or two, stop and call for help. Waiting longer lets mold start.

Common mistakes

  • Using fans without a dehumidifier, which can just move damp air around.
  • Closing the room and leaving it wet.
  • Painting or covering surfaces before they are fully dry.
  • Assuming a dry surface means a dry structure.
  • Using electrical items in standing water.

Turn off power to wet areas if you are not sure it is safe, and never stand in water near outlets or appliances.

Documenting your own drying

Even when you dry things yourself, take before and after photos and write down what you did and when. If a problem appears later, such as a stain or a smell, those notes help a professional work out what happened and help your insurer understand the timeline.

Equipment homeowners can use

ToolGood forLimit
Wet/dry vacuumStanding water on hard floors and carpetDoes not dry what is under the surface
Box or floor fanMoving air across wet surfacesNeeds a dehumidifier to be effective
DehumidifierPulling moisture from the airSmall units are slow for large losses
Moisture meterChecking walls and floorsNeeds a dry reference reading to compare
Towels and mopSmall spillsNot enough for soaked materials

Rental dehumidifiers and fans are strong enough for a small area. A flooded basement or a wet wall cavity takes more airflow and heat than household tools provide, and that is where a crew is useful.

Whatever equipment you use, measure. A meter reading before, during and after drying shows whether you are making progress, and it gives you something to show an adjuster.

If you borrow or rent equipment, place it so that cords stay out of wet areas, and plug it into a circuit that is not affected by the water. When anything electrical is near water, stop and call for help.

How long to keep trying

Give a home drying effort a day or two. If a carpet, wall or floor is still wet, hand it off. The goal is not to prove you can do everything, but to prevent mold and keep the repair small. A crew can usually take over smoothly from where you left off, especially if you kept notes and readings.

Tell the crew what you already did, including when the water started and which items you moved. That saves time and avoids repeating steps.

Drying in the Ohio Valley climate

Southern Indiana summers are humid, so opening windows can bring in damp air instead of drying a room. Run a dehumidifier and keep windows closed when the outdoor air is muggy.

In older Jeffersonville homes with basements, check the lowest level first. Water settles there, and slow drying in a basement can lead to mold in a few days.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a household fan to dry a room?

A fan helps move air, but it works much better with a dehumidifier.

How long does drying take?

It depends on the material and amount of water. Moisture readings tell you, not the calendar.

Can I dry a wet mattress?

Often not. Items soaked through are hard to dry, so ask your insurer or a professional.

Is a wet vacuum enough?

It removes standing water, but not what is in the materials below.

When should I stop and call?

When the water is dirty, when walls or ceilings are wet, or when readings do not fall.
