How to Remove Mold From Walls in Jeffersonville, IN

By the Jeffersonville Water Damage Pros team

A small spot on a painted wall is a different job from growth in the drywall. Here is how to tell, and how to clean safely.

☎ Call (812) 642-7696

Mold on a wall is usually a sign that moisture got there first. Cleaning the surface without fixing the source brings it back, and cleaning the wrong way can spread spores through the house. This guide explains what is reasonable to do yourself and what is not.

We do not give health advice, and anyone with breathing concerns should talk to a physician before cleaning mold. For large areas, jump to the mold remediation page.

When a homeowner can clean it

If the growth covers a small area, sits on a hard, painted surface and has not penetrated the drywall, careful cleaning is reasonable. If you find soft, crumbling or stained drywall, the growth has gone deeper, and cleaning will not solve it.

If the area is larger than a few square feet, if there has been a flood or sewage, or if mold comes back after cleaning, call a professional.

How to clean small areas safely

  1. Fix the leak or moisture source.
  2. Wear an N95 respirator, gloves and eye protection.
  3. Keep the area closed off and turn off fans that pull air through the room.
  4. Wipe with a damp cloth and a mild detergent solution, working from clean to dirty.
  5. Dry the wall completely and bag the cloths before disposing.

Avoid mixing bleach with ammonia or other cleaners. The fumes are dangerous. Bleach also does not penetrate porous materials, so it is not a cure for growth in drywall.

When drywall must come out

Drywall that is stained through, soft or has growth on the back of the panel is cut out. Cuts are made beyond the visible growth, and the studs behind are cleaned and dried. Plastic sheeting contains dust, and the removed material is bagged on site.

That is containment work, and it is what a crew does. After removal, the cavity is dried, and new drywall goes in. Our wall and drywall page describes the rebuild.

Preventing a repeat

Keep humidity in a moderate range, run exhaust fans and fix leaks promptly. Do not paint over mold, and use a primer only after the wall is clean and dry. Check windows and exterior walls for condensation in cold weather.

If the wall was wet from plumbing, a plumber has to repair the leak. See burst pipe damage for how water travels in walls.

What to avoid when cleaning

  • Do not dry-brush or sand mold, because that sends spores into the air.
  • Do not use a household fan that blows across the growth.
  • Do not mix cleaning products.
  • Do not seal wet drywall behind new paint.
  • Do not skip protective gear because the patch looks small.

Each of these either spreads the problem or hides it. The goal is to remove growth, remove moisture and verify that the wall stays dry.

If you land in the lower rows, the safest step is a phone call. A short description of what you see is usually enough to decide whether someone needs to visit.

After the wall is clean

Cleaning is only half the job. Once the surface looks clear, dry the wall completely and watch it over the next few weeks. A wall that is dry to the touch can still hold moisture inside, so a meter reading is more reliable than your hand.

  1. Run a dehumidifier or fan in the room, pointed so that it does not blow across any remaining growth.
  2. Check the wall again after a few days for staining or odor.
  3. Prime and paint only after the surface is clean and dry.
  4. Keep an eye on the area after heavy rain and during humid weather.

If staining or odor returns, there is still a moisture source. Look for a roof leak, a failing seal around a window, a bathroom fan that vents into the attic or a plumbing drip inside the wall. Fixing that source is what actually ends the problem, and cleaning alone will not.

Protective gear checklist

  • N95 or better respirator, fitted to your face.
  • Non-porous gloves that reach past the wrist.
  • Goggles without ventilation holes.
  • Disposable coveralls or old clothes you will wash right away.
  • Heavy trash bags for sealing cloths and debris.

Put on the gear before you open the area, and remove it carefully outdoors or in a laundry room. Wash clothing separately in hot water.

Mold on walls in older Jeffersonville houses

Older homes, including many in the Old Jeffersonville Historic District, often have plaster walls. Plaster can hold moisture and mask growth behind paint.

If you are working on a historic home, test a small area first and avoid harsh scrubbing that damages old plaster. When in doubt, ask a crew to take moisture readings before you open the wall.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use bleach on walls?

Bleach does not reach growth inside porous drywall and may leave moisture behind. Detergent and drying are the common approach for small hard-surface spots.

Will paint cover mold?

No. Painting over mold hides it and it grows through. Clean and dry first.

How do I know if mold is inside the wall?

Musty smell, stains that return, and moisture meter readings suggest it.

Do I need to leave the house?

For small areas, usually not. For large areas or anyone with health concerns, ask a professional.

What does a crew do differently?

Containment, HEPA filtration, removal and verification of dryness.
