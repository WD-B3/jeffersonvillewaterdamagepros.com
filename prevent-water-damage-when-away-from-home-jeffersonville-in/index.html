How to Prevent Water Damage While Away in Jeffersonville, IN

By the Jeffersonville Water Damage Pros team

An empty house gives a small leak days to become a big one. A short checklist before you leave lowers the risk.

☎ Call (812) 642-7696

A leak that would be a mop-and-towel problem when you are home can ruin floors, walls and ceilings when nobody is there. Water runs for days, soaks into materials and starts mold. Most of these losses come from a short list of causes, and most are preventable with a few minutes of work before you travel.

This checklist covers winter trips, when frozen pipes are the main risk, and summer trips, when appliances, air conditioners and storms take over. Pick the items that fit your home.

Before every trip

  1. Shut off the main water valve if you will be gone more than a few days, or at least the valves to the washing machine.
  2. Check under sinks, behind toilets and around the water heater for drips.
  3. Look at supply hoses on the washer, dishwasher and refrigerator for bulges or cracks.
  4. Clear gutters and make sure downspouts carry water away from the foundation.
  5. Test your sump pump if you have one, and confirm it has power.
  6. Ask a neighbor or friend to check the house on a schedule.

Write down where the main shutoff is and tell the person checking on your home. A leak found quickly by someone who knows where to turn off the water is far cheaper than a leak found after a week.

Winter trips and frozen pipes

Cold weather brings a specific risk: pipes in unheated spaces freeze, crack and release water when they thaw. Keep the heat on, set to a temperature that keeps the house well above freezing, and open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls.

  • Disconnect and drain outdoor hoses and shut off outdoor spigots from inside.
  • Insulate exposed pipes in crawl spaces, attics and garages.
  • Seal drafts near pipes in exterior walls.
  • Consider shutting off and draining the water supply if you will be gone for an extended time.

If a pipe does freeze and burst while you are away, the first sign may be a water bill or a neighbor's call. Read about frozen pipe damage and burst pipe damage so you know what the response looks like.

Summer trips

Warm weather has its own risks. An air conditioner can drip or clog its condensate drain, a washing machine hose can burst, and a storm can open a roof or flood a basement.

  • Check the air conditioner drain line and pan, since a clogged drain can overflow.
  • Replace old rubber washer hoses with braided ones before they fail.
  • Make sure the sump pump has a working battery backup if the area floods.
  • Inspect the roof and flashing before the storm season.

Our page on air conditioner leaks explains how condensate problems cause ceiling and wall damage, and appliance leak damage covers washers and dishwashers.

If you come home to water

  1. Stay out if the water is near electrical outlets or the electrical panel.
  2. Shut off the water supply at the main.
  3. Take photos and video for your insurer.
  4. Call a restoration company to start extraction and drying.
  5. Call your agent to report the loss.

The sooner drying starts, the smaller the repair. Our water extraction service page explains the first steps.

A one-page checklist you can print

  • Main water shutoff located and labeled.
  • Washer hoses checked and replaced if old.
  • Water heater and sinks inspected for drips.
  • Gutters cleared and downspouts extended.
  • Sump pump tested.
  • Heat set to protect pipes in winter.
  • Neighbor or friend has a key and the shutoff location.
  • Sensors have fresh batteries.

Tape the list inside a cabinet door so it is there before every trip. Repeating it each time takes less than ten minutes and builds the habit.

Seasonal risks around Jeffersonville

Clark County sits in a region that sees both winter freezes and heavy rain events, and the Henryville tornado of March 2, 2012 reminded the county that storms can open roofs. The National Weather Service recorded about 17 miles of tornado path in Clark County, strongest in Henryville.

Before storm season, check that roofs, gutters and sump pumps are working, and ask a neighbor to look in on the house if you leave town. If you live in or near a Special Flood Hazard Area, the city of Jeffersonville notes that a flood policy normally takes effect 30 days after purchase, so plan ahead.

Frequently asked questions

Should I turn off the water when I leave?

For longer trips it is a good idea. For short trips, closing the washing machine valves and checking hoses covers the most common leaks.

Can I leave the heat low in winter?

Keep it high enough to protect pipes. Ask a plumber or your insurer what minimum they suggest.

Do water sensors really help?

They can give you early warning, but only if someone gets the alert and can act on it.

Who should I give a key to?

Someone you trust who knows where the main shutoff is.

What if a pipe bursts while I am away?

Shut off the water, document the damage and call for extraction and drying right away.
