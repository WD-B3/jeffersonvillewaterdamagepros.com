Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Mold in Jeffersonville, IN?

By the Jeffersonville Water Damage Pros team

Mold coverage is one of the most misunderstood parts of a policy. The cause of the moisture and the wording of your policy decide it.

☎ Call (812) 642-7696

Many homeowners find out about mold coverage after finding the mold. Policies vary a lot. Some exclude mold entirely, some cover it only when it results from a covered water loss, and many cap the payout at a set amount. Reading your policy before a problem is the best protection.

We are a restoration company and not an insurance advisor, so this is general information. Your agent can tell you how your own policy reads, and the Indiana Department of Insurance can help if you have a dispute.

How insurers usually think about mold

Insurers generally treat mold as a result and not a cause. If a covered event, such as a burst pipe, led to mold and you acted promptly, mold remediation may be covered up to a limit. If mold came from a long-term leak, poor ventilation or a flood without flood coverage, coverage is often denied.

Some policies have a separate mold limit, and others exclude mold unless an endorsement was added. Look for words such as fungi, wet rot or microbial in the exclusions section.

What affects the answer

  • The cause of the moisture: sudden or gradual, covered or excluded.
  • How quickly you reported and dried the damage.
  • Policy wording on mold, fungi and wet rot.
  • Any sublimit for mold remediation.
  • Whether you maintained the home and fixed known problems.

That second item is one of the few things you control. Drying wet materials within a day or two protects both your home and your claim. See our structural drying page for how it works.

What to do if you find mold

  1. Stop the moisture source and dry the area.
  2. Photograph the growth and the damage before cleanup.
  3. Call your agent or claims line and ask about mold coverage and limits.
  4. Get a written scope of work from a remediation company.
  5. Keep receipts for the work and for any temporary housing.

Do not wait for the claim to settle before drying. Insurers expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and waiting can make the damage larger.

Lowering the odds of a denied claim

Document maintenance, such as plumbing repairs and roof work. Respond fast when you notice water. Keep copies of your policy where you can find them, and review the mold section at renewal. If your policy excludes mold and you live in a damp area, ask whether an endorsement is available and what it costs.

Reading your own policy

Pull out your declarations page and the full policy, and look for these items before you ever need them.

  • Whether mold, fungi or wet rot is excluded or limited.
  • The dollar limit for mold remediation, if any.
  • Whether the limit includes testing and repair, or only cleanup.
  • Any time limit for reporting a loss.
  • Your deductible, and whether it differs for certain perils.

If the wording is hard to follow, ask your agent to walk you through it and mark the paragraphs that matter. A twenty minute call now is easier than a dispute later.

You can also ask about an endorsement that adds or raises mold coverage. Not every insurer offers one, and the price varies, but knowing your options gives you room to decide before you have a wet basement.

Keeping good records

If you have mold, photographs and dated moisture readings help your insurer see how it started and whether you acted promptly. Keep invoices for plumbing repairs and any past leak repairs, because they show the home was maintained.

Never discard moldy materials before photographing them. If an adjuster wants to see them, keep a sample in a sealed bag when it is practical to do so.

Mold and flood questions in Jeffersonville

Jeffersonville participates in the National Flood Insurance Program, and the city is rated Level 8 in the Community Rating System, which gives a 10 percent discount on flood premiums in Special Flood Hazard Areas. A flood policy covers flood damage, and mold coverage under it follows its own terms.

The Indiana Department of Insurance says a homeowners policy will not cover flood loss, and that a flood policy normally takes effect 30 days after purchase. If your home sits near the river or in a low area, ask your agent about both.

Frequently asked questions

Is mold ever covered?

Sometimes, when it follows a covered water loss and the policy allows it, often with a limit. Check your policy.

What is a mold sublimit?

A cap on how much the policy pays for mold, separate from the main limit.

Does flood insurance cover mold?

Flood policies have their own terms. Ask your flood insurer how mold is handled.

Should I file a claim for a small patch?

Compare the cost with your deductible and ask your agent how a claim could affect your premium.

Can a restoration company tell me if I am covered?

No. We can document the loss and write a scope of work, but only your insurer decides coverage.
