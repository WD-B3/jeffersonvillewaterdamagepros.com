Water Damage Restoration in Port Fulton, Jeffersonville, IN

Port Fulton is one of the oldest named parts of Jeffersonville. If water has gotten into your home there, call and tell us what you see.

☎ Call (812) 642-7696

Port Fulton was laid out in 1835 by Victor Neff and named for Robert Fulton, the steamboat pioneer. It grew up beside the Ohio River as a place for maritime work, and the French and Howard shipyards that later became Jeffboat started here. Jeffersonville annexed the town in 1926 after residents blocked an earlier attempt in 1853, so today Port Fulton is a neighborhood inside the city.

If you are dealing with a burst pipe, a failed water heater, a roof leak or a wet basement in Port Fulton, our crew works out of Jeffersonville and answers the same 24/7 line for every address in the city.

Where Port Fulton sits

Port Fulton lies upriver from Louisville and east of the older downtown core. When the town was platted, it ran between Jackson Street on the east and Division Street on the west, and between Court and Charlestown Streets on the north. At its height it reached from the Ohio River up to about 10th Street.

What the history means for a wet house

A neighborhood platted in 1835 and filled in over many decades does not have one housing type. You will find homes from different eras on the same block, with different plumbing, different foundations and different framing. That mix matters when water arrives, because the right drying plan depends on what is behind the wall and under the floor.

  • Older supply lines and fittings can fail at joints, so a small drip can run for days inside a wall before anyone sees a stain.
  • Older houses may have crawl spaces or partial basements, and these hold moisture longer than a slab does.
  • Plaster, lath and solid wood floors dry slowly and need readings, not guesses.

We do not assume the age of your house. We look at it, take moisture readings and tell you what we find.

Flood and storm water near the river

Port Fulton is close to the Ohio River, and the January 1937 flood is a documented part of Jeffersonville history. River flooding and ordinary heavy rain are different problems. Rising river water and rain runoff are usually treated as flood by insurers, and many standard policies leave that out. Backup through a drain is often a separate endorsement. Read your policy before you assume either way.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. The FEMA Flood Map Service Center lets you search your own address, and your agent can say what applies.

What to do first if water is in your home

  1. Stop the source if you can do so safely, such as the shutoff at a fixture or the main valve.
  2. If water is near outlets, appliances or your electrical panel, stay out of the water and do not touch a breaker while standing in it. Call an electrician if you cannot reach power safely.
  3. Move valuables out of the wet area, but photograph the damage first.
  4. Call us. Tell us where the water came from and how long it has been there.

If the water came from a sewer or drain backup, keep children and pets away and do not try to dry or clean it yourself. That water can carry contamination and needs proper cleanup. See our sewage backup cleanup page for how that is handled.

Extraction, drying and documentation

We remove standing water with pumps and extractors, then set up air movers and dehumidifiers and check moisture in walls, floors and framing. We keep checking until the readings match dry areas of the house, not just until it looks dry. For a deeper look at the process, see structural drying and dehumidification.

For your insurance claim, we record photos, moisture readings and a written scope of work. We do not decide coverage. Your insurer does, and the paperwork helps your adjuster see what happened.

Frequently asked questions

Do you cover Port Fulton?

Yes. We take calls for Port Fulton as part of our Jeffersonville service area. Call the 24/7 line, give your street and tell us what you see.

Is Port Fulton part of Jeffersonville?

Yes. Jeffersonville annexed Port Fulton in 1926, so it is a neighborhood within the city and falls inside our service area.

Is my Port Fulton home in a flood zone?

Does my homeowners policy cover river flooding?

Often not. Many homeowners policies exclude flood and want a separate flood policy. Your agent can tell you what yours says.

What if the water came from a sewer backup?

Keep children and pets out of the area and leave the cleanup to professionals, because that water can be contaminated. Call and we will explain what happens next.

How soon should drying start?

Once the source is stopped. Mold can start within days on wet materials, so do not wait for the claim to be settled.
