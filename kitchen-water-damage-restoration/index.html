Kitchen Water Damage Restoration in Jeffersonville, IN

Kitchens have more water lines per square foot than any other room. Learn how cabinets, floors and toe-kicks are dried and repaired.

☎ Call (812) 642-7696

A kitchen has a sink, a dishwasher, often a refrigerator ice maker and a lot of supply lines packed into a small space. A slow drip under the sink can soak cabinet bases and subfloor for weeks before anyone notices. By then, the cabinet floor is swollen and the toe-kick is stained.

This page covers the kitchen room itself, including cabinets, countertops and floors. The appliance that failed is covered on appliance leak damage, and a burst supply line is on burst pipe cleanup.

Signs of kitchen water damage

  • Swollen or warped cabinet bottoms and doors.
  • Dark stains or a musty smell under the sink.
  • Cupped floorboards or lifting vinyl near the dishwasher.
  • Soft spots in the subfloor.
  • Bubbling paint or stains on the wall behind the sink.

What to do right away

Close the shut-off valve under the sink or at the appliance, then empty the cabinet and photograph it. Use towels for small spills and a wet vacuum if you have one.

If the leak ran for a long time, assume that the toe-kick, the subfloor and the wall base are wet. A moisture meter will tell you how far it spread.

Cabinets: dry, repair or replace

Solid wood cabinets can often be dried and saved. Particleboard and MDF swell when they get wet and rarely return to their shape. Cabinet boxes that stayed wet for days are usually replaced, while doors and face frames in good shape may be kept.

Toe-kicks and base trim are removed so the cavity behind them can dry. Countertops over wet cabinets are lifted only if moisture is trapped.

Flooring in the kitchen

Tile dries well but the grout and underlayment need checking. Vinyl and laminate trap water, and hardwood cups. Our hardwood floor page describes what happens when wood floors get wet.

After drying, a drying process confirms the cavity is back to normal before cabinets and floors are reinstalled.

Living in the home during repairs

Plan for noise and equipment in the kitchen for several days. Keep food and medicine away from the wet area, set up a temporary prep space and keep children and pets clear of the drying equipment. We explain how long we expect each stage to take as the readings come in.

Where kitchen water goes

Kitchens are dense with water lines and appliances: sink supplies, dishwasher hoses, refrigerator ice maker lines and disposals. A leak under a sink can run along the cabinet floor, into the subfloor and under adjacent flooring.

  • Behind and under the dishwasher.
  • Under the sink cabinet and in the cabinet toe kick.
  • Beneath the refrigerator where the ice maker line runs.
  • Inside walls along supply lines.
  • In the ceiling below when the kitchen is on an upper floor.

We check each area with a moisture meter, because the cabinet may look fine while the subfloor beneath it is wet.

Cabinets and countertops

Solid wood and plywood cabinets often dry well. Particleboard swells and rarely returns to shape. We pull toe kicks and open backs where needed so air reaches wet areas, and we tell you early if cabinets will need replacing.

Countertops are rarely harmed by water, but the substrate under laminate can be. Photograph everything for your insurer before work begins.

Dealing with the appliance

If an appliance caused the leak, shut off its supply and unplug it if safe. Keep any failed hoses or parts, because an insurer or manufacturer may want them. Our page on appliance leak damage explains this in more detail.

Insurance and your policy

Sudden leaks from kitchen plumbing are commonly covered, while long-term seepage from a leaking trap or faucet is often excluded. Cabinet replacement may be treated differently from repair.

Photograph under the sink before cleaning and keep the failed part.

Frequently asked questions

Can wet cabinets be saved?

Solid wood often can. Particleboard that swelled usually cannot.

How do I know if the subfloor is wet?

Soft spots, odor and a moisture meter reading tell you.

Do I need to remove the countertop?

Only if moisture is trapped below it.

Can mold grow under the sink?

Yes, if it stays damp. See mold remediation if you see growth.
