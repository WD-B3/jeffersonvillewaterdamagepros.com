How we handle each town

Clarksville sits beside the older part of Jeffersonville, so our crews know the area and the sewer and water service that serve it. Sellersburg has its own building department and utility pages, and we point callers to the town for permit questions. Memphis and Henryville are census-designated places in Clark County, so permits and zoning questions go to Clark County Planning and Zoning.

Each town page explains the local contacts and what to do before the crew arrives.

For Clarksville, we note the July 2023 water main break and boil advisory that Indiana American Water reported around West Washington Street, north McKinley Avenue and north Randolph Avenue, because a utility event can change water pressure in nearby homes. For Sellersburg, we note the town building department at 316 E Utica Street, (812) 246-3821, and the Storm Water Ordinance the town adopted in January 2021.

For Henryville, we note the March 2, 2012 EF4 tornado, which the National Weather Service tracked along about 17 miles in Clark County, because storm damage often becomes water damage when roofs open.

When you call, give us your street address even if you use a mailing address in another town. Clark County has several communities that share ZIP codes or mailing names, and the street address tells us where the crew is going. If the address is hard to find, tell us the nearest cross street or landmark.

We do not promise an arrival time, because roads, weather and call volume change from hour to hour. What we do promise is that someone answers, that you get safety steps on the call and that we explain the scope before work starts. If you are outside the towns above, call anyway and we will tell you plainly whether we can help.

Before you call

Have a few facts ready so the call goes quickly. Your street address, where the water came from, which rooms are wet and how long it has been there are the most useful. If you are not sure of the source, say what you see and smell.

Tell us if the water is dark or has an odor, since that points to a drain or sewer and changes the protection we bring. If anyone in the home is elderly, ill or very young, tell us so we plan around them.

If you can do so safely, shut off the water at the main valve and keep clear of wet outlets, the electrical panel and any appliance sitting in water. Take photos and video of the damage before anything is moved, and write down the time you found the water.

Those simple steps help both the crew and your insurer, and they take only a few minutes. Our town pages list the contacts and steps that apply where you live, and our guides explain insurance, mold and leak detection in plain language. If you rent, tell your landlord right away as well, since the lease may require notice, and keep copies of every message you send.

If you are unsure whether a problem is an emergency, call anyway. A short conversation costs nothing, and it is better to ask early than to let water sit overnight and find mold a week later. We would rather talk you through a small problem than meet you after a large one.

Landmarks and neighborhoods we know

Jeffersonville includes Port Fulton, the downtown and Riverfront area, the NoCo Arts and Cultural District and Oak Park, which was annexed in 2008. Landmarks include the Old Jeffersonville Historic District, the Big Four Bridge, Warder Park, the Howard Steamboat Museum, the Quartermaster Depot, Norton Clark Hospital and the Spring Street Freight House.
