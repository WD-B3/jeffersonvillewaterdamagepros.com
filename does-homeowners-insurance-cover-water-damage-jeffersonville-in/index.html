Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage in Jeffersonville, IN?

By the Jeffersonville Water Damage Pros team

Sometimes yes and sometimes no. The cause of the water decides almost everything, and here is how to read your policy before you file.

☎ Call (812) 642-7696

Most homeowners ask this question on the worst day, with water on the floor and a deductible in mind. The honest answer is that it depends on where the water came from and how fast it arrived. Insurers sort water losses into sudden and accidental events, which are often covered, and slow, long-term or outside events, which often are not.

We are a restoration company, not an insurer or a lawyer, so treat this as general information and read your own policy. Your agent or the Indiana Department of Insurance can answer questions about your specific coverage.

What is usually covered

A supply line that bursts, a water heater that fails or an appliance hose that splits is the kind of sudden and accidental loss that standard homeowners policies are written for. Damage from the water is commonly covered, subject to your deductible, while the failed part itself is often not.

The same logic applies to water that enters after a covered storm opens the roof. A wind or hail event that damages the roof, followed by rain coming in, is treated differently from a roof that leaked for years. See our page on storm water damage for how that cleanup works.

What is usually not covered

  • Flood damage from rising water, which needs separate flood coverage.
  • Slow leaks and seepage that went on for weeks or months.
  • Damage tied to lack of maintenance.
  • Sewer and drain backup unless you added an endorsement.
  • Groundwater that seeps through foundation walls.

The Indiana Department of Insurance states that a homeowners policy will not cover flood loss. Sewer or drain backup coverage is usually an add-on, and the Department notes it is often capped at a low amount.

Why the cause matters so much

Two basements can look identical with two inches of water, and one claim is paid while the other is denied. A burst pipe is a plumbing event. Water coming up through the floor after a long rain is groundwater, and water rising from a drain is a backup. Each has its own rule.

That is why documentation matters. Photos, moisture readings and a plumber's note about the source help your insurer see what happened. We keep readings and a written scope for this reason, and we never promise how a claim will turn out.

What to do before you file

  1. Stop the water and protect the property from further loss.
  2. Photograph and video everything before cleanup.
  3. Read the water damage and exclusion sections of your policy.
  4. Call your agent and ask what is covered and what your deductible is.
  5. Keep receipts for emergency repairs and temporary housing.

If the repair cost is close to your deductible, a claim may not be worth filing. Your agent can talk through how a claim could affect your premium. Our water extraction page explains what the first hours look like.

Mistakes that make claims harder

  • Throwing away damaged items before photographing them.
  • Waiting days to stop the water or start drying.
  • Letting the claim sit while mold begins to grow.
  • Signing a repair contract before you know what your insurer will approve.
  • Assuming a verbal answer from an agent is the same as written coverage.

Most of these come down to timing and records. If you are unsure, call your agent first and the restoration crew second, or do both at once, since drying should not wait on paperwork.

Jeffersonville flood insurance facts

Jeffersonville participates in the National Flood Insurance Program and holds a Community Rating System rating that gives a 10 percent discount on flood insurance premiums for policies in Special Flood Hazard Areas, according to the city. Flood insurance is required for federally backed mortgages in those areas.

Check the FEMA flood map for your address, and keep in mind that the Indiana Department of Insurance notes a flood policy normally does not take effect until 30 days after purchase. Waiting until the river rises is too late.

Frequently asked questions

Does insurance cover a water heater that bursts?

Often the water damage is covered while the water heater itself is not, but check your policy. Our water heater leak page explains cleanup.

Is a basement flood covered?

Usually not if it comes from groundwater or a drain backup, unless you have flood or backup coverage. A sudden plumbing failure may be covered.

Do I need flood insurance in Jeffersonville?

Lenders require it for federally backed mortgages in a Special Flood Hazard Area. Even outside those areas, a lender not requiring it does not mean your property cannot flood.

Will filing a claim raise my premium?

It can. Ask your agent before you file.

Where can I check my own flood zone in Jeffersonville?

Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center or ask the city Building Commissioner's office at (812) 285-6412.
